# MRS. WRASSE'S CHANDLERY: Mrs. Wrasse, a dry, brisk heron in round
# spectacles and a cream apron, runs Saltcombe's chandlery: rope, oilskin, lamp oil,
# candles, and anything else a boat or a person might need to get through a
# wet week. She knows the price of everything and does not haggle. She is
# kind the way a good coil of rope is kind: reliably, and not out loud.
# Each section is one line of the shopkeeper's, shown in the shop's text box.

[talk: Who are you?]
Wrasse. The Chandlery. My mother had it, her mother had it, and her mother's mother is the reason we have the lease. I count the stock on Sundays. I'd count it on Mondays too, if it weren't my day off.

[talk: The oilskin]
Waxed twice a year, the old way. The new stuff is cheaper and wears out quicker, and I don't sell it. I like my customers to come back alive.

[talk: The lamplight]
That's Mrs. Tansy's work, on the quay. She has her rounds, I have mine. Nobody lights the ones up the hill. She isn't fussed about it. I'm not fussed about it. Mind the oil, that's all.

[talk: Bramble]
The small one with the axe. A boat axe, for ropes. I'm told he's afraid of the sea. Good, sensible lad. I'm afraid of the sea. It keeps my customers coming in.

[talk: Ves]
She asked if I stock umbrellas. I stock sailcloth. Same thing, if you're serious about it. She nodded as if I'd said something clever.

[talk: The Hero]
He came through here once, in my great-gran's day. Small. Bought a coil of rope and nothing else, and paid in coppers, one at a time. It's in her ledger. We don't throw out ledgers.
