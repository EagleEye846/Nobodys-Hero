# THISTLE: keeps Lasthome's gate. An old marmot in a herald's coat that was
# red once and has been dyed brown, with a pike he uses to hang the washing
# on. His great-great-grandmother came down the far side of the mountain from
# Heralds' Rest. He has never once asked anybody if they're the Hero.

[first]
Evening. Healer's on the left as you go in, green door. Bryony. She'll see to the girl.

Go on. And wipe your feet.

[repeat]
Wipe your feet. That's the only rule I keep, and I keep it fierce.

[first after the_healer]
She'll mend. Bryony's mended worse. Bryony mended me, the winter I fell off the roof.

What was I doing on the roof? Hanging the washing. Don't ask.

[repeat after the_healer]
Go and get some stew in you. Mabb's, by the inn door. You look like the fen chewed you up and spat you out.

[first after ves_on_her_feet]
Up and about, is she? Good. I'll take my hat off to her. I would, if I had a hat.

The notice? The ridge. The birds went quiet up there two days back, and they've not started again. Last night they stopped down here and all, for a bit. I've seen birds go quiet before. Never two days.

[repeat after ves_on_her_feet]
Keep your doors shut at night. That's all I'm saying. That's all anybody's saying.

[first after bramble_goes_home]
Corran'll see him right. Corran's never lost a sack of charcoal in his life, let alone a boy.

Funny little thing. Shouted at me the whole way out the gate. Said he'd be BACK.

[repeat after bramble_goes_home]
He'll be back. They always say that. Sometimes they are.

# --- SIDE QUEST: Thistle's hat (it's on the washing line in the square) ---

[hat_ask]
Thistle straightens his coat and looks up at the sky, which is a hole in the roof.

Had a hat. Herald's hat, felt, brass badge, handed down. Wind took it off the gate Tuesday week. It went towards the square. Hats do.

I'm not asking. I'm saying.

[hat_wait]
Felt. Brown. A brass trumpet badge. If you see a hat doing nothing, it's probably mine. Don't go to any trouble.

[hat_done]
Thistle holds the hat out at arm's length. It's damp, and quite clean, which it hasn't been since before he was born.

Corran's line. Of course. Corran washes anything that stands still.

He puts it on. He takes it off. He puts it back on, straighter.

There. Now I can take it off to people.

He reaches inside his coat and brings out a brass button, stamped with a trumpet. His thumb has worn the trumpet nearly flat.

My great-great-gran gave this to my gran, who gave it to me. It's off a herald's coat, from Heralds' Rest. She walked down the far side of the mountain when the waiting got to be too much. She said it's a fine thing to wait for someone, but not on a stair.

Take it. I'd rather it went somewhere than hung on a nail with the rest of us.

[hat_after]
Thistle tips his hat at you. It's a very good tip. He has clearly been practising it in his head for years.
