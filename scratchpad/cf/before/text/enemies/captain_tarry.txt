# Shown at the start of each of your turns, in order, then repeating.
[flavor]
CAPTAIN TARRY stands at a chart table with nothing on it. His finger follows a course that was rubbed off the paper long ago.

His boots never touch the deck. The deck creaks anyway, politely, for him.

Somewhere below, a voice says "Aye, Captain." It is not the same voice twice, and it is not alive.

He looks at the stern windows. It is always just before dawn in them.

Smells of lamp oil, wet wool and a closed room.

[flavor: mutiny]
CAPTAIN TARRY keeps looking over your shoulder, at the door, for a crew that has stopped answering.

His cutlass shakes in his fist. He has not once, in a hundred years, wanted to use it.

The ship is full of letters, and not one has been sent.

[flavor: heave]
CAPTAIN TARRY has taken his hat off. Without it he looks tired, and a good deal younger.

The deck is leaning. He leans with it, the way a man does who has stood on a hundred years of night watch.

The bell is tolling by itself, slowly, the way it would for port.

# One section per TALK option. The names after "talk:" must match the
# TALK options in src/data/enemies/captain_tarry.js.
[talk: Inspect]
CAPTAIN TARRY - HP 42000
Master of the brig Fair Return, which never made port. Draws a cutlass, runs out the guns, shouts orders through a trumpet and throws the last of his rum; then his crew boards you, with letters, and lines come over the rail; at the end the deck lists, and the bell tolls. He is not cruel. He gave his word to bring them home.

[talk: Ask the day]
You point at the log on the table, then at the sky in the stern windows, and mime a question: what day is it? CAPTAIN TARRY opens his mouth, and says it is the forty-first. He says it very confidently. Then he says it is the forty-first again, a little more slowly, and frowns at the table as if it had moved.

[talk: Read him a letter]
You take one of the letters from the crew's lockers and read it aloud: a mother's hand, a Tuesday long gone, "the garden is coming on, and the kettle is always on, come home when you can." CAPTAIN TARRY goes quite still. He asks whose it is. When you say, he takes off his hat, and puts it on again, and does not trust his voice.

[talk: Show him the log]
You open the log on the chart table to the last page and hold it up. It says "Day 41. Port tomorrow." And above it "Day 41. Port tomorrow." And above that, all the way back, the same line, in the same hand, over and over, a hundred years of pages. CAPTAIN TARRY reads one. He reads the next. He puts a finger on the paper and it goes through.

[talk: Hold up the compass]
You take out the brass compass from the chart table and hold it up. The needle does not point north. It does not point anywhere. It turns, slowly, toward the stern windows, and then toward you, and then it settles on the floor, like a thing looking for ground. CAPTAIN TARRY says it has been doing that since the night of the storm, and that he has always meant to have it seen to.

[talk: Ask who is left]
You gesture at the cabin, at the quiet ship below it, and at the doorway where nobody is standing. Who is left to give orders to? CAPTAIN TARRY says the crew, of course. Then he calls for Mr Pike. And for Hobbs. And for the boy, whose name he has lost. Nothing answers but the deck, and it is very quiet, and he stands in the quiet for a while.

[talk: Say it plainly]
You step up to him and say it with your hands, plainly, as kindly as you know how: the ship went down. A long time ago. Everyone aboard went down with her, and you are all still here because nobody told you to go. CAPTAIN TARRY looks at you for a long time. Then he asks, in a very small voice, how long. You hold up your hands, and then you keep holding them up.

# How the fight ends. The rewards line is added automatically.
[victory]
CAPTAIN TARRY sinks down at the chart table, his hat on his chest, his eyes shut. "Not yet," he says. "One more tide." The cabin stops leaning, and the bell stops tolling, and the room is very quiet.

[pacified]
CAPTAIN TARRY puts down the trumpet and takes off his hat. He straightens, with a great effort, and turns to the stern windows, where it is always just before dawn.
