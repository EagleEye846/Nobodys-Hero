# THE OLD DRAINS: under Hollin's well (Chapter 3). Hollin's brick cistern and
# culverts, built down onto the old city's great stone water-conduit, which
# runs on under the wood to where it breaks out into the marsh. Things you
# can read and look at. See notes/docs/chapter-3-drains.md.
#
# A section with `_bramble` on the end of its name is what Bramble says
# after the section of that name, if he's with you (see OverworldScene.say).

# What a save point says when you save here.
[save_point]
The crystal glows in the dark, and the water nearby glows with it. Down here, that's a lot.
Your progress is saved.

[save_failed]
The crystal flickers, but nothing happens...
(Your browser isn't letting the game save.)

# --- Things all through the drains ---

[current]
Fast, shallow water, racing along its channel.

(Step in and it carries you, one way only, till something stops you. Stopped, you can step out sideways.)

[current_bramble]
{glee}It's like a slide! A wet slide! Grandad never said there were SLIDES.

[cask]
A cask, lying on its side, hooped with rusty iron. It washed down from Hollin's cellars some time in the last fifty years.

(Walk into it to push it. It rolls till something stops it, rides the currents, and wedges in deep water for you to step on.)

[cask_bramble]
{grin}Barrels are just logs that have been to the village. I can do barrels.

[drain_water]
Water, deep and cold, standing in the channel.

[drain_grate]
An iron grating across the culvert, rusted through at the bottom. The bars there have been bent apart. Something small has been squeezing through.

You won't fit.

[drain_grate_bramble]
{proud}I will. I fit through EVERYTHING. (Press Q.)

[root_plug]
Roots have grown down through the bricks and right across the culvert, as thick as your arm. There's a draught coming through them.

[root_plug_bramble]
{determined}Roots are just trees that went the wrong way. Stand back, Sword.

[roots_open]
{grin}CHOP! ...And chop. And chop. There. Roots.

[rubble]
Bricks and stones fallen out of the roof. Nothing goes past them: not water, not you.

[silt_heap]
A heap of grey silt. There's a button in it, and a thimble, and something that might have been a spoon.

[silt_heap_bramble]
{thinking}Everything that ever went down Hollin's drains is in here somewhere. Grandad lost a marble down the well once. He cried about it for a WEEK.

[rock_stump]
An old stone post, worn smooth on top. Press on it and everything in here goes back where it started.

[rock_stump_bramble]
{sheepish}For when you get it wrong. Not that I would.

[rock_home]
Everything you've pushed rolls and bumps back to where it started.

[chest_empty]
Empty.

[gold_pouch_empty]
An empty purse.

# --- 1. THE CISTERN ---

[cistern_chalk]
A chalk arrow on the bricks, east. Underneath, smaller:

"it's not following. I think."

[cistern_chalk_bramble]
{worried}She thinks. She THINKS.

{thinking}...It didn't follow us either. It just looked. Like it was waiting for something.

[cistern_sign]
A wooden board, carved, black with damp:

HOLLIN CISTERN. KEEP IT SWEET.
Wheel lets the basin down. Mind the trench, it's deep.

[cistern_wheel]
An iron wheel on the sluice. Something heavy shifts under the water when you turn it, and the basin starts to drain.

[cistern_grating]
High overhead, a grating, and a little grey daylight coming down through it: the square, up there.

Something passes over the light. The grating goes dark for a long, long moment.

Then it's light again.

[cistern_grating_bramble]
{scared}...

{scared}We're not here. We're NOT here. We're under the ground and it can't see under the ground.

{determined}Keep going, Sword. Quietly.

[cistern_vault]
The vault of the cistern, brick on brick, all of it sweating.

[cistern_wall]
Old red brick, wet through. There's a line of green on it where the water used to stand.

[cistern_trench]
A trench down the middle of the basin, black and deep. You can't see the bottom.

[cistern_basin]
The floor of the basin, slimy with fifty years of not being looked at.

[cistern_chest]
A jar of jam, sealed with wax, that somebody hid here a long time ago. "MAGS", it says on the lid.

[cistern_purse]
A little purse, wedged in the bricks. Somebody's savings, dropped down the well.

[gratings_sign]

GRATINGS TO BE CLEARED EVERY SPRING.

[gratings_wheel_ledge]

[gratings_wheel_far_bramble]

[gratings_gate]
An iron gate, shut. It's worked from somewhere else.

[gratings_gate_open]
With a groan, the gate swings open.

[gratings_wall]
Brick, slimy and cold. The gratings are rusted into it.

[gratings_purse]
A purse in a nook in the wall.

[gratings_chest]
A chest, hidden in a nook. The lid's stuck, then isn't.

# --- 4. LADLE'S NOOK ---

[ladle_fire]
A little fire in an old iron grate. It's the warmest thing for a mile.

[ladle_kettle]
A kettle, always just about to boil.

[ladle_cot]
A bed of folded sacks, very neat. You could sleep here.

[ladle_crates]
Crates of things Ladle has fished out of the water and sorted: spoons, buttons, a doll, three left boots.

[ladle_barrel]
A barrel of clean water. Ladle's.

[ladle_gutter]
A gutter of running water along the wall, clear as glass. It's the only clean water you've seen down here.

[ladle_wall]
The bricks here are swept, and somebody has whitewashed a bit of them, a long time ago.

[ladle_chalk]
A chalk arrow by the far way out. Next to it, small, a note:

"(he's nice) (say thank you)"

[ladle_chalk_bramble]
{awed}...She said thank you. Even running away from a monster, she said thank you.

{proud}I'd have said thank you as well.

# --- 6. THE OLD CONDUIT ---

[conduit_chalk]
A chalk arrow on the old stone, low down. The line wobbles.

[conduit_chalk_bramble]
{worried}It's all wobbly. Her hand was shaking.

{determined}...Then we'd better hurry up.

[conduit_sign]
There's writing cut into the stone, but it isn't any writing you know. Under it, scratched much later, in letters you do know:

WOODCUTTERS GO NO FURTHER

[conduit_sign_bramble]
{sly}Grandad's people put that there. Grandad's people never went ANYWHERE.

[conduit_carving]
A carving, worn nearly smooth by water: a mountain, and water running down from it in wavy lines, all the way to a city with walls round it and a great gate.

[conduit_carving_bramble]
{awed}That's here. That's this. They made the water go to their city, on purpose.

{thinking}...What city?

[conduit_carving_2]
The same carving: the mountain, the water, the city, the gate. Somebody carved it over and over, all the way along.

[conduit_weir]
A great iron wheel, worked into the old stone. It turns the weir. Somewhere, the water changes its mind.

[conduit_current]
The great current, racing down its channel.

(Step in and it carries you. You can't walk against it.)

[conduit_still]
Deep water, held still behind the weir, waiting.

[conduit_channel]
A channel of the old conduit, cut out of the living stone.

[conduit_wall]
Old, old stone, the same as the Ruins are made of. The water's worn it smooth up to a line, and above the line it's green.

[conduit_pool]
A pool where the floor has fallen in. Deep.

[conduit_service_lever]
A lever by a little door in the wall. It's stiff, but it goes.

[conduit_service_gate]
A narrow door in the stone, iron-bound. A passage up the side of the conduit, for whoever looked after it, once.

[conduit_service_open]
The little door grinds open. There's a way back up the side, now.

[conduit_breach]
A hole in the old stone, bricked up long ago, badly. The bricks are cracked, and a cold draught comes through.

[conduit_breach_bramble]
{sly}That wasn't bricked up very well. Somebody was in a hurry.

[conduit_breach_rubble]
Bricks, fallen out of the patch in the wall.

[conduit_purse]
A purse, on a ledge of the old stone.

[conduit_chest]
A chest, set back in a niche.
