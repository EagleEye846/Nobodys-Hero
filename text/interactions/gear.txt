[read_log_page_1]
FAIR RETURN, brig, 214 tons. A. Tarry, master. Saltcombe for the Brightwater roads, and home.

DAY 1. Sailed on the morning tide, wind fair at north-east. The whole town on the quay to see us off, my wife at the end of it with her hat in her hand and the little one on her shoulder.

Every berth full. Mr Pike mate. Hobbs at the masthead, where he would sleep if I let him. Billy, ship's boy, his first voyage, sick as a dog before we cleared the point and proud of it after.

The men have named the figurehead Madam Fairweather. New gilt on her, her chin up, her hand held out for a coin. Every man touches it going forward. I have said nothing. I touched it myself.

Due home the 14th. I have promised it.

[read_log_page_2]
DAY 19. Lying in the Brightwater roads, waiting on our cargo. No shore leave. The post boat has not come out to us, so the men's letters stay in the purser's bag. They will post them themselves at home, they say, and save the stamps.

Aboard: a clock, a kettle, six dozen glass floats, a rocking horse. The horse is not on the manifest. I will square it with the owners.

Billy asked me how you know the way home. I showed him the binnacle. He asked what you do if the compass fails. I told him, then you find your way by the stars. He was up half the night learning them.

[read_log_page_3]
DAY 38. Homeward. Wind foul and backing, the glass falling all day.

Hobbs swears he saw a small figure on the cliff road at dusk, on its own. Nobody walks that road. I have doubled the watch.

Mr Pike has the men singing at the capstan, the old one, Port Tomorrow, and they will not sing the last word of it till we are in. The bosun says it is bad luck to sing "home" at sea. I let them have it.

Three days. Port tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow.

[read_log_page_4]
DAY 41. Fog since the forenoon, thick as wool. Lost the coast at noon.

In the last dog watch a light came down out of the sky ahead of us, white, burning, slow as a feather, and struck Madam in the breast. The men cried out. When I came forward her face was wet and she was smiling. I will swear she was not smiling before.

Later. Struck on the bar. Taking water fast. I am tearing out these pages to go ashore in bottles, so the owners will know the fault was not the men's. They did everything.

Tell my wife I was nearly in. Tell Billy's mother

[read_log_page_5]
DAY 41. Fog. Lying off the point, waiting on the bell to bring us in.

They hung a bell for us on the point, and they rang it faithful, every fog, and every fog we answered and stood in close. And they would not come down to the water. I do not understand it.

Tonight there was no bell. I put the glass on the point: four men and a lantern, at the bottom of the ebb, taking our bell down off its post. They took her tongue out first, so she could not call us. They carried her to the old boat on the point, and down the steps under it, and along under the cliff to the rock where the fog-watch used to stand, and hung her there, where nobody would ever ring her.

A mistake ashore. It will be put right. Port tomorrow.
