# --- THE GARDEN AND THE JETTY ------------------------------------------------------

[door_sign]
A board on a post: HADDOCK'S LIGHT. LIGHTMAN IN. KNOCK HARD, HE'S DEAF.

[door_sign_ves]
{bored}Knock hard. Right.

[door_lantern]
A lantern on a post by the door, lit. Somebody has trimmed the wick this very evening. Somebody always does.

[door_bottles]
Bottles on posts, a message in each. They all say COME HOME.

[door_bottles_bramble]
{sad}...Bit sad for a garden.

[door_buoys]
A pile of buoys, painted and repainted. HADDOCK, they say, and HADDOCK, and HADDOCK.

[door_pots]
Crab pots, stacked in a tidy tower. One has a flower growing out of it.

[door_barrel]
A rain barrel. It is full, because it is always full.

[door_rope]
A coil of rope on the jetty's approach, flaked down neatly.

[door_lifebuoy]
A lifebuoy on a post, scrubbed white. The rope on it is new.

[door_bother]
The Bother, a stout little rowboat with a blue stripe, moored to the jetty. The oars lie shipped, ready, and there is a bailing tin in the bottom.

[door_pouch]
A purse in the flowerbed, tucked in among the posies where somebody hid it from the gulls.

[door_rock]
The rock the light stands on, flat as a table and scoured white by the spray.

[door_sea]
The sea, grey-green and heaving, all the way round the rock.

[door_wall]
The tower's white brick, whitewashed every year. It curves away overhead into the rain.

[door_window]
A window low down in the tower, with a warm lamp in it.

[door_niche]
A little lamp, in a niche in the brick.

[door_turf]
A garden on a rock: turf, flowers, and a good deal of salt.

[door_path]
A gravel path, raked.

[door_planks]
The jetty's planks, scrubbed pale.

# --- THE PARLOUR ---------------------------------------------------------------------

[parlour_cot]
A cot against the wall, the blanket pulled up tight. A walrus-sized dent in the pillow.

[parlour_stove]
A round iron stove with a kettle on it, ticking. The kettle is off the boil, as always, in case anybody comes.

[parlour_tides]
A tide board, chalked up to the day. Underneath, in different chalk: STILL HERE.

[parlour_ledger]
HADDOCK'S LIGHT. Lit, lit, lit, lit, lit...

One night, nothing is written at all.

[parlour_crate]
A crate of spare wicks, labelled in careful capitals.

[parlour_barrel]
A barrel of lamp oil. It smells of paraffin and biscuits.

[parlour_lifebuoy]
A lifebuoy on a post, and a mug hung on it. Somebody has given up on hooks.

[parlour_wall]
Round white brick, whitewashed, with a tide board chalked up on it.

[parlour_window]
A window on the sea. It's too dark out there to see anything but the rain.

[parlour_floor]
Warm boards, worn pale in a path from the chair to the door.

# --- THE STAIR -----------------------------------------------------------------------

[stair_marks]
Two sets of marks scratched in the whitewash, side by side, with years beside them.

One climbs higher. The other stops.

[stair_marks_bramble]
{sad}...He was littler than me.

[stair_crate]
A crate of lamp-glass, packed in straw.

[stair_barrel]
A barrel of oil, lashed to the rail so it can't roll down.

[stair_rope]
A coil of rope, left on the stair. Somebody has been up and down with it, and not put it away.

[stair_wall]
Whitewashed brick, curving away round the stair.

[stair_window]
A narrow window on the storm.

[stair_niche]
A little lamp in a niche, lit, to light the stair.

[stair_floor]
Worn boards, each step hollowed in the middle.

# --- THE LITTLE ROOM ----------------------------------------------------------------

[room_bed]
Made. The sheets are new.

[room_drawing]

One says ME. The other says THE HERO.

[room_drawing_ves]
{thoughtful}...Good lighthouse.

[room_boat]
A little wooden boat. Someone has painted BOTHER II on the side.

[room_chest]
A sea chest at the foot of the bed. Inside, folded and aired, there is a LIGHTMAN'S COAT: heavy oilskin, patched at both elbows, smelling of paraffin.

[room_wall]
White brick, curving in. Someone has dusted it.

[room_window]
A small window, and a clean sill, and the sea.

[room_floor]
Scrubbed boards. Nothing on them, anywhere, is out of place.
