# --- THE TOWER'S OWN WALLS ----------------------------------------------------------

[ll_wall]
Black brick, ribbed with iron bands, sweating in the damp. It leans in as it goes up.

[ll_niche]

[ll_window]

[ll_floor]

[ll_pillar]

# --- THE SLUICE ROOM -----------------------------------------------------------------

[sluice_crank]
A great iron crank on the wall, a wheel as big as a cartwheel with a handle as long as your arm, geared down to a chain that runs off into the dark.

You put your weight on it. It comes a quarter turn, then tries to take your arm back. It won't stay up on its own.

[sluice_crank_ves]
{thoughtful}It only stays up while somebody leans on it.

[sluice_crank_bramble]
{worried}Nope. Not touching it. It looks like it bites.

[sluice_gate]
The sluice gate: an iron plate in a frame, raised a hand's breadth, with green water standing behind it. The plate is red with rust, except along the bottom edge, where it has been scraped bright.

[sluice_lamp]
A lamp on a post, burning low.

[sluice_grille]
An iron grille across the passage, hung on three chains that run up into the roof. Each chain is hauled by a weight, and each weight hangs from a plate in the floor.

[sluice_grille_open]
The three weights hold, and the grille slides up into the roof, dripping.

[sluice_wheel_hero]
A chain wheel beside the grille, turning very slowly as the weight above it settles.

[sluice_plate_hero]
An iron plate in the floor beside the grille, worn bright. It wants a body on it.

[sluice_wheel_bramble]
A little chain wheel in a bricked-up corner, set low.

[sluice_plate_bramble]
A plate in the floor of the little room, with a boy-sized dip worn in the middle.

[sluice_gap]
A gap low in the little room's wall, where an old pipe came through. Too small for you.

[sluice_gap_bramble]
{proud}I fit. I always fit. It's my best thing.

[sluice_wheel_ves]
A chain wheel on the south wall, its chain running up the wall and across the roof.

[sluice_plate_ves]
A plate in the floor, scrubbed bright by boots. It is a long way from the grille.

[sluice_sign]
A board on a post: THREE WEIGHTS, THREE PLATES. STAND ON ALL THREE, THEN GO. DO NOT STEP OFF UNDER THE GRILLE.

[sluice_barrels]
Barrels of lamp oil, stacked two high against the wall.

[sluice_crate]
A crate of chain links, packed in grease.

[sluice_rope]
A coil of rope, stiff with salt.

[sluice_pouch]
A purse in the corner behind the crate, where the draught has not got at it.

# --- THE GALLERY ---------------------------------------------------------------------

[gal_circle]
A circle chalked on the brick, two rings with little waves round it and a boat in the middle. Drawn, rubbed out and drawn again. The lines are shaky and very careful.

[gal_circle_ves]
{thoughtful}Somebody's been practising.

[gal_scorch]
A circle burnt into the brick. The ring is black and the middle is scorched through to the iron. This one worked, a bit.

[gal_scorch_bramble]
{worried}A bit?

[gal_sums]
Sums, chalked in a child's hand: tide times, high and low. The answers are wrong, crossed out, and wrong again, and right at last. An arrow up. An arrow down.

[gal_hand]
Handwriting, big at the start and small at the end, as if the chalk was running out of wall: WHEN I GET IT RIGHT THE SEA WILL STAY. WHEN I GET IT RIGHT HE WILL COME BACK.

[gal_hand_ves]
{thoughtful}...Right.

[gal_hand_sorry]
SORRY, written down the brick, smaller each time, until the last one is only a dot.

[gal_hand_sorry_bramble]
{worried}He says sorry a lot. To a wall.

[gal_floor_circle]
A big circle on the floor, with a paper boat at each of its four points. The boats are dry. They have been dry a long time.

[gal_table]
A small table with a stub of chalk worn to nothing, a jar of seawater with a paper boat sunk in it, and a stack of tide tables, each one marked up in a different colour.

[gal_chair]
A chair with its legs sawn short, so a small boy's feet reach the floor.

[gal_boat]
A paper boat, folded very well, sitting on the floor. It is dry.

[gal_driftwood]
Driftwood, stacked by size, the straightest pieces set aside. Staffs, maybe.

[gal_lime]
A barrel of chalk and lime, nearly empty. A great deal has been used.

[gal_oilskin]
A yellow oilskin on a coat stand, far too big for any child. The sleeves would hang past a boy's hands twice over, and the hem is standing in a puddle of its own dripping.

It was cut from memory by someone who remembered how big the coat looked from underneath.

[gal_oilskin_ves]
{thoughtful}Somebody else's coat, drawn from memory.

[gal_oilskin_bramble]
{worried}He'd trip on that. He'd trip on that every day.

[gal_lamp]
A lamp on a post, burning low by the east door.

[gal_pouch]
A purse under the last of the chalk circles, in the corner. It has been there some while.
