# MATTOCK: a brown mole in a red hard hat and a blue neckerchief, the other
# miner left at the pithead. A talker. Staying "until it goes properly dark".

[first]
Don't mind Sedge. He's been foreman for forty years, and he's decided we're all still on shift.

I'm Mattock. I'm staying till it goes properly dark. Then I'm going home. It's a plan.

[repeat]
My grandad's grandad dug with the Hero, you know. Well, near the Hero. In the same mountain.

He said the Hero never took a pick. Just walked down the old workings like they owed money. And he said the Hero was small.

Nobody believed him. We all thought the Hero would be, you know. Bigger.

He swore he saw a star come down once, too. Straight through the roof of the Old Seam and on down, into Seam Nine.

Seam Nine's not on any map. They shut it up after, and hung a sign on the lever saying it was broken. Funny thing to do with a broken lever.

Oh, and if the postman's still after me, it'll be from Mum. I can't read, so I keep them in my hat. It's a very full hat.

[first after the_light_goes_up]
It's not going to go properly dark now, is it? I said I'd stay till it did. That's a good sort of problem to have.

I'll go home. After a bit.

[repeat after the_light_goes_up]
My grandad's grandad said the Hero was small. I'll tell his headstone he was right.

# --- SIDE QUEST: the letter from his mum (see bt_vendor_beetle) ---

[letter_in]
Mattock takes his hat off and shakes it. A shower of letters falls out, dozens of them, some very old, and he points at one on top with a muddy paw. That one's got a postmark on. That's new.

You take it. You read it to him, all the way through, while he sits on an upturned bucket with his ears flat.

"Dear Mattock. It's Mum. The roof is fine. The hen is fine. Your dad says stop digging. I say dig if you like, but eat something. There's a stew. It's been on since March. Come home when it's dark, or when it isn't. I'll leave the door on the latch. Love, Mum. P.S. Empty your hat."

Mattock wipes his face on his neckerchief. On the latch. She's left it on the latch since I was four.

Tell her... no. I can't write. Hold on.

He presses his thumb in the dirt, and then on a scrap of sacking, and then, carefully, draws a hat. That's "soon". She'll know.

You take the reply.
