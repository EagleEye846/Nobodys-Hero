# HORACE: Head Herald of Heralds' Rest (the fourth to hold the title; they
# were all called Horace), in the square by the brazier. A sash, a hat with a
# feather, a moustache you could hang a coat on, and a speech he has been
# practising since before he was born.

[first]
Ahem. AHEM. Positions, everyone! Positions!

...Where is everyone. Never mind. I, Horace, shall do it myself.

HERO! WELCOME TO HERALDS' REST, where for one hundred years we have kept the soup hot, the beds made and the trumpets... hm.

You're not quite... that is to say... the prophecy mentioned a cape. And a certain height.

No matter! NO MATTER. You came up the stairs, and that's more than anyone's done in a very long time, and I for one say WELCOME.

The Hall is open, the inn is warm, the stores' prices are what they are. And if you'd like to be announced at the feast, I have a speech. It's about four hours.

Oh, and do look in at the chapel. Young Ves is in there, praying, as ever. She has been asking about the stairs.

[repeat]
I've been Head Herald for thirty years. My father before me, and his before him. All called Horace. It saves repainting the sign.

The speech, if you're wondering, begins "O HERO" and ends, some time later, with a fanfare. I've never had a chance to say the middle bit aloud.

Between you and me: I don't mind if you're the Hero or not. I'd just like to say the speech to somebody, once.

Mind Nan, the old goat. She bites. Affectionately.

[first after ves_in_the_chapel]
Ahem! HERO! And... young Ves! Together! Splendid. SPLENDID.

Horace, Head Herald, at your service. I always said that girl would go up the stairs one day. Well. I said it to Nan. Nan bit me.

Keep each other warm up there. And do come back down for the speech. It's still about four hours.
