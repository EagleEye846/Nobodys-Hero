# REST STORES: Fennick, the storekeeper of Heralds' Rest. Spectacles, a long
# scarf, and a ledger a foot thick.
# Each section is one line of the shopkeeper's, shown in the shop's text box.
# Sections starting with "talk:" become topics under TALK, in this order.

[greeting]
Welcome to Rest Stores. Fennick's the name. Prices firm, prices fair. You'll be the one from the stair. Everything's in stock. Everything's ALWAYS in stock. That's rather the problem.

[talk: Who are you?]
Fennick. Stores. Third generation. My grandmother opened this shop the week the village was built and sold nothing for eleven years, which is a record I'm trying not to beat.

[talk: The village]
Built to welcome the Hero. A hundred years ago. Every herald you've met is the grandchild of someone who came up here to blow a trumpet once and then had to find something to do with the rest of their life.

[talk: The Hero?]
Prophecy says a Hero climbs the stair and the sun touches the summit and so on. Didn't say when. We assumed "soon". We assumed wrong. The Head Herald still assumes.

[talk: The Keeper]
The old man with the lantern? Came in off the stair an hour before you did, calm as you like, and asked for a cup of tea. Said he'd walked. Walked! Up the ICE! Didn't have a snowflake on him.

[talk: Skill points]
The Head Herald's idea. The heralds have a store of... I don't know what to call it. Pluck. Saved up a hundred years with nothing to spend it on. Five hundred gold a point, and only a couple each, so the village has some left for the Hero. Whoever that turns out to be.

[talk: Selling]
I buy. Half what it's worth, since I'll never sell it on. Can't buy what you're wearing; take it off first. And I don't buy soup. The inn would know.

[talk: What's up there?]
Past the Lookout? Cloud, then stair, then more cloud, then, they say, the top. Nobody from here has been and come back. One of ours got to the second cairn and came back quiet. I sold her the mittens. Didn't help.

[talk: Umbrella parts]
Ribs, handles, canopies. The lamplighter bought her spares here every autumn for twenty years. I kept ordering them after. Habit. Somebody's bound to need them, I said. And look.
