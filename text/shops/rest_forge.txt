[talk: Who are you?]
Tilda. My grandad was the smith before me, and his mother before him. One of us has kept this fire going every day for a hundred years. Hot enough to forge a sword at a moment's notice.

[talk: The forge]
Why do I cook on it? Because it's lit. You don't heat two fires on a mountain. Bread on the back shelf, stew on the hook, iron in the middle. Never mixed them up. Nearly, once.

[talk: The Hero's sword]
Grandad forged a sword for the Hero. A hundred springs we've sharpened it. Then one night, door bolted, fire lit, and by morning the hooks were bare. Only a black smudge on the stone where it hung. Shaped like a hand.

[talk: The Hero?]
Prophecy said the Hero would need a blade at the top. So we waited with one. A hundred years of sharpening, and now it's gone. Whoever has it, they'll find it very sharp.

[talk: Daggers?]
A merchant came up the stairs once with a pack bigger than my anvil. Traded me the glass dagger and the iron gauntlets for a week of stew. Fair trade. That pack ate most of the stew.

[talk: Ves]
The girl from the chapel? She brings me that umbrella whenever a rib bends. Her mother's. Won't let anyone else touch it. I've made her a steel tip for it and a cape for the sparks, if she ever goes anywhere that needs them.
