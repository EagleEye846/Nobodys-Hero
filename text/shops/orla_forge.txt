[talk: Who are you?]
Orla Cobb. Smith. I light the forge from the big light's oil, and the big light's lit from my forge. Don't ask which came first. It's a loop. I like it.

[talk: The Isles]
Little lights, all in a row. Every one of them was somebody's job once. Now they're mine to mind, mostly, when I remember. I don't always remember all of them.

[talk: The lamp oil]
Burns clean. Burns hot. Burns a long time. Everything I make with it keeps its edge. I don't know why. I used to ask why. Now I just pour it.

[talk: Haddock]
That walrus owes me a boathook. Tell him when you see him. Tell him I'm not cross. Tell him I'm a little cross.

[talk: Bramble]
That boy's gripping his axe like it might swim away. Good. Keep a grip. The sea doesn't give things back, as a rule.

[talk: Ves]
She asked if I do umbrellas. I don't. I do iron. But I had a canopy frame that might do, if she can stand the noise. The storm one. It sings in the wind.

[talk: The Hero]
A small lad, once. Came through here a hundred years back, when it was my gran's forge. Stood at the door looking up at the light. Didn't come in. Gran thought about that more than she'd say. The door was open.

[talk: The old light]
The one on the shelf? Nobody's used that in years. The draught up there keeps a wick going for days, if somebody's left one. And the tea'll be the crab-pot lot. They go up to look at the weather. They'd leave their own heads up there.
