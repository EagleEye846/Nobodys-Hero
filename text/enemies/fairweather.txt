# MADAM FAIRWEATHER, THE FIGUREHEAD: the secret superboss of chapter 5, at the end of the ghost
# ship's deep part. The carved wooden woman on the brig Fair Return's prow, woken by a piece of the
# Fallen Star that lodged in her breast a hundred years ago. On the Fair Return's last good night
# she made one wish on it: that the night would never end. The star granted it to the letter, so
# it is a party nobody can leave, and she can't stop laughing. She isn't mad or possessed; she is
# stuck inside that wish. Sparing her lets it go (morning can come); beating her breaks it.
# She laughs, she spins, she is glad of anyone at all, and she doesn't know her own strength. She speaks in bright, broken
# fragments, like a person at a party who has been alone for a very long time. The hero never speaks.

# Shown at the start of each of your turns, in order, then repeating.
[flavor]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER hangs out over the water on her own prow, one hand held out for a coin that has not been given since before the ship sank.

Her laugh goes out over the sea in rings you can almost see. The waves answer it a moment late.

The star in her breast beats like a second, quicker heart. The wood round it is cracked, and the cracks are full of light.

Her hair is carved in thick ribbons and painted walnut. The salt has had the paint off every edge.

She smells of old varnish and a clean night with the frost coming.

Out past her the night goes on, and on. There is no grey in the east at all.

[flavor: shower]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER's hand can't hold the light in any more, and the stars have noticed. Some of them are coming down to see.

The sky over the perch is much too full. It keeps adding one.

[flavor: carousel]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER is turning, and the deck heels over under her. Somewhere a barrel organ plays a waltz that has lost its way home.

The painted horses have got loose from nothing and are riding round on gilt poles. They look delighted. Their eyes are empty.

[flavor: chaos]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER has come apart down the middle, and every piece of her is laughing at a different time.

It is a party, and it is the whole sky, and it is all at you.

The light in her cracks has stopped being white and turned a pink you do not like at all.

[flavor: spareable]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER has stopped spinning. She hangs on her prow, chin lifted, the star beating slowly, looking at you as a figurehead looks at the horizon.

# One section per TALK option. The names after "talk:" must match the TALK options in
# src/data/enemies/fairweather.js.
[talk: Inspect]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER - HP 62000
The brig Fair Return's figurehead, woken by a splinter of the Fallen Star in her breast, on a night she wished would never end. Foam, splinters, her laugh in rings; then falling stars and comets; then the carousel and the maypole; then all of it at once. She isn't cruel. She is playing, and nobody has told her how hard.
Her deck lists, and rolls over when she laughs. And she will want to dance.
Everything she throws blinks first. Nowhere in the box stays safe.

[talk: Laugh along]
You laugh. It isn't much of a laugh, but it's yours. MADAM FAIRWEATHER stops, astonished, and then laughs so hard she has to hold on to the rail. For a moment the rings of her laughter go out softer than before.

[talk: Dance with her]
You hold out your arms, and the two of you turn round on the prow, badly, under the sky. She leads. She is a figurehead and has never danced. She is thrilled. When the waltz runs out, she is still turning, but more slowly.

[talk: Catch a star]
A star comes down, a small one, and you catch it, and it burns your hand for exactly as long as it takes to be surprised. You put it in your pocket. MADAM FAIRWEATHER claps her carved hands, and the clap goes off over the sea like a gun.

[talk: Hold out a hand]
You hold out a hand, palm up, the way hers has been held out for a hundred years. She looks at it. She looks at hers. She puts something into yours. It is nothing, and it is gold, and it weighs exactly what a coin does.

[talk: Ask her name]
You point at her, and then at yourself, and shrug: who are you? She says it is Madam Fairweather. She says it twice, in two different voices, one of them younger. She says the carpenter chose it, for luck, and that the ship had a good deal of that, at first.

[talk: Name the ship]
You gesture up at the masts behind her, the rail, the bow, and she follows your hand. You say, with your lips, the way a sailor would: Fair Return. MADAM FAIRWEATHER goes quite still. "Fair," she says, "Return." She says it again, as if trying the weight. "Was that her name?" She has been out here in front of it all this time, and she has never been able to read it.

[talk: Say goodbye]
You say it with your hands, plainly, as kindly as you know how: you have to go, and she can stop now. She doesn't have to keep the party up for anyone. The star in her breast beats once, slowly, and then once more. MADAM FAIRWEATHER looks out at the sea she has been showing off to for a hundred years. "Is it morning?" she says. And then, quieter, in her own voice: "...Oh. It's very quiet."

# How the fight ends. The rewards line is added automatically.
[victory]
The pink goes out of the cracks, and then the white. MADAM FAIRWEATHER stops laughing in the middle of a breath, and stays where she is, a carved figurehead on a prow, her chin lifted, her hand held out. The star in her breast is only a dull stone now.

[pacified]
MADAM FAIRWEATHER lets her hand fall. She hangs on her prow a moment longer, smiling, looking out to where the morning is going to be. The star in her breast goes down, slowly, like a lantern turned low.
